Zoho Assist alternative: Auffi compared head-to-head

Of all the commercial remote support tools, Zoho Assist is the one closest to Auffi: in both, the person helping works in the browser and the person getting help starts a small program. So the difference is less about technology and more about the model. Zoho Assist is a commercial helpdesk suite with technician accounts and plans. Auffi is open source (AGPL-3.0), needs no account for ad-hoc help, is free for business use too and can be self-hosted — with servers in Germany.

When Auffi is the better choice

  • You want to help without an account — neither you nor the other person signs up for anything.
  • You help commercially but without a budget: Auffi has no technician licenses and no limit on parallel sessions.
  • Open source and self-hosting matter to you, for example because your company may not route screen data through third-party clouds.
  • You need a German service without third-party trackers, without first reviewing a data processing agreement with a non-European corporation.

When Zoho Assist is the better choice

  • You run a helpdesk with several technicians and need session recording, queues, reports or your own branding.
  • You need to control or view Macs, Android devices or iPhones — Auffi cannot do that.
  • You already use Zoho Desk or other Zoho apps and want to start remote sessions straight from a ticket.

Zoho Assist alternative: where the difference shows up day to day

On paper the two tools look alike: help from the browser, a small program on the other end, a session number to connect. So people looking for a Zoho Assist alternative rarely want a completely different way of working — they want a different business model.

The account. In Zoho Assist every session starts in the technician console, and that only exists with a Zoho account. For a company with a fixed support team that is no problem. For the neighbor who wants to help her father over the phone, it is: she has to sign up before she sees a single screen. With Auffi she opens auffi.app, types the 9-digit code her father reads out, and is connected.

The cost. Zoho Assist has an honest free plan: one technician, one session at a time, with no time limit on desktop computers. As soon as a second colleague wants to help, or you want to run two sessions in parallel, the paid plans begin, billed per technician. Auffi has no plans at all — not for individuals, not for businesses, and the number of helpers does not matter.

The data. Zoho offers data centers in the EU, but it remains a corporation headquartered in India and the US whose privacy policy allows processing outside the EU as well. Auffi runs on a server in Frankfurt, stores no IP addresses in clear text and sends video and input end-to-end encrypted over WebRTC. If you need to be strict about it, run Auffi on your own server — the source code is public on GitHub.

Consent. With Auffi the person sharing their screen confirms every ad-hoc session with a click. Without that confirmation nobody sees anything. For your own machines there is unattended access with an account and a per-device password — but that is an add-on, not a requirement.

To be honest: what Auffi cannot do

Auffi is deliberately lean and not a helpdesk suite. There is no session recording, no queue for incoming requests, no reports for team leads and no ticketing integration. The Auffi sharer runs only on Windows and Linux; Macs, Android devices and iPhones cannot be controlled. You can transfer files during a session; beyond that there are no extras.

If your day consists of many tickets, several technicians and a mixed device fleet, Zoho Assist is the better-suited tool. If, on the other hand, you are looking for a Zoho Assist alternative for the moment someone needs help unexpectedly and nobody wants to deal with sign-ups, plans or cloud contracts, Auffi is built exactly for that. The two do not exclude each other: many IT teams use a suite for the helpdesk and a lean tool for everything that comes up unplanned.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Auffi and Zoho Assist?

In both, the person helping works in the browser and the person getting help starts a small program. The difference: Zoho Assist is a commercial helpdesk suite in which every helper needs a Zoho account. Auffi is open source (AGPL-3.0), needs no account at all for ad-hoc help and is free for business use too.

Is Zoho Assist free?

Zoho Assist has a free plan with one technician and one concurrent session. For more technicians, parallel sessions and features such as session recording you need one of the paid plans, which are billed per technician. Auffi costs nothing in any case, businesses included.

Does the person being helped have to install anything with Zoho Assist?

On Windows, according to Zoho's help pages, they download and run ZA_Connect.exe after entering the session ID; on a phone an app is required. Auffi is similar: the person sharing their screen starts the small Auffi sharer for Windows or Linux. On the helper's side Auffi needs nothing but a browser.

Can I self-host Zoho Assist or Auffi?

Zoho Assist is a cloud-only service run by Zoho. Auffi can be run entirely on your own server: signaling server, TURN server and web front end all start via Docker Compose.

When is Zoho Assist the better choice?

When you run a helpdesk with several technicians and need features such as session recording, queues, branding, reports or remote access to smartphones and Macs. Auffi deliberately does not offer these.
