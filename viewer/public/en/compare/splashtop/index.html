Splashtop alternative: Auffi compared head-to-head

Splashtop SOS is a polished, paid helpdesk tool: an annual license per concurrent technician, a technician account and an app on both ends — in return you get broad platform coverage and team features. Auffi is free, open source (AGPL-3.0) and self-hostable. The person helping just opens auffi.app in a browser and types a 9-digit code — no installation, no account. If you are looking for a Splashtop alternative for ad-hoc help without managing licenses, this is the honest comparison.

When Auffi is the better choice

  • You help occasionally — family, a club, a few customers — and do not want to pay a license per technician.
  • The person helping should not install anything or create an account: a browser is enough.
  • You want to run the service yourself, or you care about open source and servers in Germany.

When Splashtop is the better choice

  • You run a professional IT helpdesk with several technicians and need recording, chat and ticketing integrations.
  • You have to support Macs, iPhones or Android devices — Auffi cannot do that.
  • You want vendor support and a contract rather than an open-source service you are responsible for yourself.

Splashtop alternative: where the difference shows up day to day

Splashtop is not a bad product — quite the opposite. Splashtop SOS is well made, fast and built for IT departments. That is exactly where the reasons for looking at a Splashtop alternative come from.

The licensing model. Splashtop charges per concurrent technician, billed a year in advance — according to the vendor there is no monthly plan. For a helpdesk team running sessions every day that is a fair, predictable cost. If you only help someone a few times a month, though, you pay a whole year's license for a handful of sessions. Auffi costs nothing, for business use too, and there is no license that expires.

Who installs what. With Splashtop SOS the technician installs the Splashtop Business app (or uses the Chrome browser) and signs in with their account; the person on the other end downloads the SOS app and reads out a code. With Auffi only the person showing their screen downloads a small sharer. The helping side opens a website, types nine digits and is connected — on any device with a current browser, without signing in.

Control over the infrastructure. Splashtop is a cloud service run by a US company. It says it has additionally operated a data center in Germany since 2020, and for organizations that cannot use the cloud at all it sells a separate On-Prem product. Auffi is open source from the ground up: signaling, the TURN server and the web interface run on your own server via Docker Compose, and the source code is public on GitHub.

What Auffi cannot do — and why that can be fine

To be honest: Auffi covers only part of what Splashtop offers. There are no mobile apps, no Mac sharer, no ticketing, no session recording and no management of large device fleets. Unattended access to your own devices is possible, but it needs an account and a password per device.

In exchange, Auffi is hard to beat for ad-hoc help: the person on the phone does not need to understand anything beyond "open the website and type the code". The sharing person has to confirm every ad-hoc session explicitly, and the connection is encrypted with DTLS-SRTP. That is why plenty of teams combine both — Splashtop for the managed fleet, Auffi for everything unplanned that comes up in between.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Auffi and Splashtop?

Splashtop SOS is a paid helpdesk product with an annual license per concurrent technician, a technician account and an app on both ends. Auffi is free, open source (AGPL-3.0) and self-hostable; the person helping just opens auffi.app in a browser and needs no account.

Is Auffi a free Splashtop alternative for businesses?

Yes. Auffi is free for commercial use too, with no per-technician license and no subscription. If you want more control, run Auffi on your own server with Docker Compose.

Does the person being helped have to install anything with Splashtop?

Yes. With Splashtop SOS the person downloads the SOS app and reads a 9-digit code to the technician. With Auffi the person sharing their screen also installs a small sharer (Windows or Linux); the helping side, however, only needs a browser.

Does Auffi support Mac, iPhone and Android like Splashtop?

No. With Auffi you can share a screen from Windows and Linux only, and there are no mobile apps. Splashtop supports far more platforms, including macOS, iOS and Android. Helping with Auffi, on the other hand, works from any device with a current browser.

Where is the data stored with Auffi and with Splashtop?

Splashtop is a US company and says it has additionally run a data center in Germany since 2020. Auffi's public service runs in Frankfurt, uses no third-party trackers and stores no clear-text IP addresses; when self-hosted, all data stays on your own server.
